# White Horse Reservoir: project context

Background for the tracker app. Status as at 1 November 2026. Figures come from Thames Water documents and press coverage unless stated. Treat cost and date figures as moving targets.

## The project

- **Name**: White Horse Reservoir. Renamed in January 2026; previously South East Strategic Reservoir Option (SESRO), and widely called the "Abingdon reservoir".
- **Promoter**: Thames Water, with Affinity Water and Southern Water.
- **Type**: fully embanked, off-river storage reservoir. It holds about 150 billion litres and covers about 6.7 km². The embankment is on average about 21 m high.
- **Location**: about 5 km south-west of Abingdon. It is bounded by the River Ock (north), the A34 and Steventon (east), the Great Western Main Line (south), and the A338 and East Hanney (west). The draft Order limits cover about 37 km², mostly in Vale of White Horse, with the eastern edge near Culham in South Oxfordshire.
- **How it works**: water is pumped from the Thames near Culham in winter and released back to the Thames in drought. There are also direct transfers to Southern Water (via a new treatment works on site) and to Farmoor.
- **Cost**: Thames Water's estimate is £5.5bn to £7.5bn, up from £2.2bn. The main works contract tender is valued at £5.7bn. Other figures quoted include £6.6bn (press) and £9bn (GARD, unverified).
- **Delivery**: a separately licensed Infrastructure Provider would design, build and finance the reservoir (the Tideway model), and Thames Water would operate it.
- **Planning route**: Development Consent Order (DCO) for a Nationally Significant Infrastructure Project. Planning Inspectorate reference WA010005. The Secretary of State for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs decides.

## Timeline

- 2006 to 2011: an earlier Upper Thames reservoir proposal was rejected after a public inquiry.
- 2024: non-statutory consultation. Water Resources Management Plan (WRMP24) approved with the reservoir included.
- 28 October 2025 to 13 January 2026: statutory consultation.
- May 2026: passed RAPID gate three, the regulators' funding checkpoint.
- 9 September to 7 October 2026: targeted consultation on access points, which rose from 6 to 17.
- 1 October 2026: three joint ventures shortlisted for the main works. Contract award expected autumn 2027.
- November 2026 (expected): DCO application submitted. Not submitted as at 1 November 2026.
- 2027 to 2028 (estimated): acceptance, examination (up to 6 months), recommendation and decision.
- 2029 or 2032: main construction start. Thames Water's own statements conflict.
- About 2040: first water. Main works complete about 2043.

## Construction impacts

- **Length**: construction would last more than ten years. Core hours are 08:00 to 18:00 on weekdays and 09:00 to 13:00 on Saturdays, with 24/7 working for some activities.
- **Main entrance (A1)**: on the A415 Marcham Road, carrying about 60% of construction traffic. It becomes the permanent visitor entrance.
- **Other access points**: 16 further temporary points on the A338, A417, A415, Drayton Road, the Steventon to East Hanney road and others. See `access-points.csv`.
- **Haulage**: East Hanney Parish Council minutes (14 October 2026) report Thames Water saying it will use the public roads (A415, A417, A338, A34) rather than internal haul roads. Rail support from Network Rail is limited, so road haulage is expected to rise.
- **Rail siding and materials handling facility**: off the main line about 1 km south of East Hanney.
- **Demolition**: 20 homes, 9 farm complexes, three solar farms, Steventon Depot, Landmead airstrip, and the existing Steventon to East Hanney road. The road would be replaced by about 5 km of new road north of the railway.
- **Watercourses**: Mere Dyke and Cow Common Brook diverted, and the River Ock realigned.
- **River Thames**: intake and outfall near Culham. Emergency discharge into the Thames is a major concern for Abingdon, Culham and Sutton Courtenay.
- **Recreation**: lakes, a water sports centre, a nature education centre, about 1,300 parking spaces, and up to about 1 million visitors a year.

## Positions

- **Opposed or seriously concerned**: Vale of White Horse District Council, South Oxfordshire District Council (emergency discharge), Oxfordshire County Council (said it cannot support the scheme in its current form, and called for the DCO to be delayed), Abingdon Town Council, MPs Olly Glover, Layla Moran and Charlie Maynard, GARD, CPRE Oxfordshire, and many parish councils. A joint parish survey across eight parishes was launched in October 2026.
- **Supportive**: Thames Water and its partners, the national government (the Chancellor listed it among nine new reservoirs in January 2025), and the White Horse Reservoir Growth Coalition, a business group launched in September 2026.

## How residents can take part in the DCO

1. Sign up for Planning Inspectorate email updates on the WA010005 project page.
2. Once the application is accepted, register a Relevant Representation within the advertised window, which is at least 30 days. Only registered Interested Parties can make written submissions throughout the examination and speak at hearings.
3. Ask parish, district and county councils to raise local issues. Councils' Local Impact Reports carry weight in the examination.
4. Contact MPs and councillors. See `representatives.csv`.
5. Speak at council meetings. The rules for each principal council are in the JSON.

## What councils must publish (England)

- **Transparency Code for Smaller Authorities**: applies to parish councils and parish meetings with turnover up to £25,000. They must publish online, free of charge:
  - draft minutes of formal meetings within one month of the meeting;
  - agendas and papers at least three clear days before the meeting.
- **Local Government Act 1972, section 228**: any local government elector for the area can inspect and copy the minutes of a parish council or parish meeting. The council is not obliged to supply copies.
- **Freedom of Information Act 2000**: anyone, wherever they live, can request recorded information. The reply is due within 20 working days. Councils must also keep a publication scheme, normally the ICO model, which covers minutes.
- **Environmental Information Regulations 2004**: reservoir information is environmental. Requests can be made verbally. Thames Water is covered by EIR but not by FOI.
- **Principal councils** (district and county): agendas must be published five clear days before a meeting. Minutes stay open to inspection for six years.

These rules are a basis for a "request missing minutes" feature in the app. Escalation is to the Information Commissioner's Office. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Local government reorganisation

- **Decision**: on 16 July 2026 the government chose three unitary councils for Oxfordshire and West Berkshire.
- **Where the affected area goes**: every reservoir-affected place falls in the new Ridgeway Council.
- **Dates**: elections in May 2027, then the new councils take over on 1 April 2028.
- **Councils abolished**: Oxfordshire County Council, Vale of White Horse and South Oxfordshire.
- **Parish councils**: continue unchanged.
