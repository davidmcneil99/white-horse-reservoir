The scheme is almost certainly **Cearn Solar Farm**. It is a roughly 2,800-acre (about 1,133 ha), up to 500MW solar and battery Nationally Significant Infrastructure Project (NSIP) proposed by Cearn Solar Farm Ltd, a wholly owned subsidiary of Telis Energy UK. It is spread across three development areas: south of Garford, east of Charney Bassett, north of West Hanney, south of Goosey and north-east of Baulking.

It is at the early pre-application stage:
- Planning Inspectorate case EN0110044;
- EIA scoping requested in October 2026;
- wider public consultation planned for early 2027;
- DCO application targeted for about September 2027.

Together with the consented 30MW Manor Solar Farm about 1km west of West Hanney, it explains the "almost surrounds West Hanney" description.

## TL;DR

- **Track Cearn Solar Farm (PINS ref EN0110044) as the main solar project.** Its developer is Telis Energy UK. It would cover about 2,800 acres with a grid offer of up to 500MW, battery storage, and an underground cable about 12km north-east to National Grid's proposed Farmoor 400kV substation (the Botley West substation). No application has been submitted, and the Energy Secretary, not the Vale, will decide.
- **The next real deadline is 23 November 2026**, for consultee responses on the scope of Cearn's Environmental Statement. Residents next get to comment directly at the developer's Stage 2 consultation, forecast for February to March 2027. Formal Relevant Representations open only after an application is accepted, likely late 2027.
- **Cearn is not the reservoir's solar relocation scheme.** No source links it to the three solar farms Thames Water dropped plans to relocate. It does sit close to the White Horse Reservoir, though. The developer lists cumulative effects with the reservoir as a key issue, and the Vale council leader has publicly warned that construction periods could overlap.

## 1. Scheme identification and overview

### 1.1 The main scheme: Cearn Solar Farm

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | Cearn Solar Farm ("Cearn" is the old name for the River Ock) |
| Applicant | Cearn Solar Farm Ltd, founded by and wholly owned by Telis Energy UK |
| Parent | Telis Energy Group, a European platform targeting a 10GW pipeline by 2030 (developer claim) |
| Named staff | Senior Development Manager named in the 3 September 2026 launch release |
| Consultants and landowners | Not published |
| Capacity | Grid connection offer of up to 500MW (up to 143,000 homes, developer claim). The Programme Document says over 100MW installed |
| Area | About 2,800 acres (about 1,133 ha) across three development areas. Not all would be panels |
| Components | PV panels, on-site substation, battery energy storage system (lithium iron phosphate expected), collector compounds, access tracks, fencing, landscaping and mitigation |
| Grid | Underground cables to NGET's proposed Farmoor 400kV substation, about 12km north-east. Connection date and queue position not published |
| Private wire | Early feasibility study of a private-wire supply to an unnamed local business hub |
| Consenting route | DCO under the Planning Act 2008, examined by PINS and decided by the Secretary of State for Energy Security and Net Zero |
| PINS reference | EN0110044. Inception meeting 4 August 2026 |

**Location and affected area.** The developer describes three development areas around Garford, Charney Bassett, West Hanney, Goosey and Baulking. Its constraints work says:
- the site has no statutory or local landscape designations;
- most of it is in Flood Zone 1, with some parcels in Flood Zones 2 and 3;
- it is partly on provisionally Best and Most Versatile soil;
- it is close to several listed buildings.

**Caveat on "almost surrounds".** The text descriptions place Cearn land north of West Hanney and further west towards Goosey and Baulking. Whether Cearn land also lies east or south of the village is not confirmed. Residents' impression probably reflects three schemes together:
- Cearn, to the north and west;
- Manor Solar Farm, to the west;
- the existing solar farms on Hanney Road, to the east.

## 3. Construction and permanent impacts

**Construction.** No construction programme, access points, lorry numbers, compounds, working hours or traffic management plan have been published. The Vale leader asked how construction traffic would be managed if the reservoir is being built at the same time.

**Permanent and operational impacts (what is known):**
- **Landscape:** the land is relatively flat. The developer promises a landscape-led design. The Vale refused the nearby Manor scheme partly on views towards the North Wessex Downs. Impacts on the West Hanney, Goosey and Baulking conservation areas have not been assessed.
- **Agricultural land:** partly provisionally Best and Most Versatile, with surveys under way. CPRE Oxfordshire's position is that Grades 1, 2 and 3a should be avoided.
- **Rights of way:** a constraints plan has been published; effects are not assessed.
- **Flood risk:** some parcels are in Flood Zones 2 and 3.
- **Biodiversity net gain:** promised, with no percentage given.
- **Glint and glare:** no assessment has been published.
- **Noise:** from inverters, transformers, the substation and the batteries. To be assessed.
- **Battery fire safety:** a safety management plan will be prepared with Oxfordshire Fire and Rescue Service.
- **Community benefits:** a fund is promised, with no amount. No shared ownership offer.

**Arguments for:**
- national clean power targets for solar by 2030;
- the Critical National Priority status of low-carbon generation in national policy;
- energy security;
- reversibility;
- biodiversity gain.

**Arguments against:**
- sheer scale;
- loss of possible Best and Most Versatile farmland;
- the landscape and heritage setting of small villages;
- stacking with the reservoir and Botley West;
- parcels in flood zones;
- battery safety;
- loss of local decision-making.

## 4. Relationship to the White Horse Reservoir

- **Overlap with the reservoir Order limits:** not established. The cable corridor towards Farmoor is the main uncertainty.
- **Not a relocation scheme:** Cearn is a separate commercial NSIP with its own transmission connection.
- **Cumulative assessment:** Cearn's Programme Document lists cumulative effects with the White Horse Reservoir as a key issue. Whether the reservoir's Environmental Statement includes Cearn is unknown.
- **Construction overlap:** likely. The reservoir's main works start in 2029 or 2032; on a typical DCO timetable, Cearn's construction would follow a decision in about 2028 to 2029.
- **Who has raised the combined impact:** the Vale council. No GARD statement was found.
- **Other cumulative developments:** Botley West and the Farmoor substation, East West Rail, the Thames to Southern Transfer, Manor Solar Farm, and Grove and Wantage housing.
