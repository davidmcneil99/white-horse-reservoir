The scheme

  • Capacity: Grid connection offer of up to 500MW (developer says up to 143,000 homes). Programme Document states over 100MW
  • Area: About 2,800 acres (about 1,133 ha) in three development areas. Not all would be panels
  • Components: Solar PV, on-site substation, battery energy storage (lithium iron phosphate expected), collector compounds, access tracks, fencing, landscaping
  • Grid: Underground cable about 12km north-east to National Grid's proposed Farmoor 400kV substation (also needed by Botley West). Cable route to be shown at Stage 2. The PINS boundary GeoJSON extends north-east towards Farmoor, so it appears to include the cable corridor
  • Location wording: South of Garford, east of Charney Bassett, north of West Hanney, south of Goosey and north-east of Baulking (PINS and developer)
  • Affected parishes named by vale: Garford
    Charney Bassett
    West Hanney
    Goosey
    Baulking
  • Land: Partly provisionally Best and Most Versatile. The amended Vale motion describes it as mostly Grade 2 and 3a
  • Flood: Mostly Flood Zone 1, some parcels in Flood Zones 2 and 3 (River Ock and tributaries)
  • Not yet published: Construction period, access routes, HGV numbers, compounds, working hours, operational lifespan, BESS size, community benefit amount, scoping report
  • Community benefit: Community Benefit Fund promised, value not stated. No shared ownership offer
  • Boundary geojson: https://national-infrastructure-consenting.planninginspectorate.gov.uk/projects/EN0110044/download-boundary

Links to the White Horse Reservoir

  • Summary: Separate projects with separate promoters and decision-makers (Cearn: DESNZ; reservoir: Defra). Cearn is not the reservoir's solar relocation scheme. They are linked through cumulative impacts
  • Overlap of sites: Not established. Cearn lies north and west of West Hanney; the reservoir lies east of the A338. The Cearn cable corridor towards Farmoor could cross the A415 or A338 corridors near the reservoir; to be confirmed when the Stage 2 route is published (early 2027) by overlaying both PINS boundary files
  • Cumulative assessment: Cearn's Programme Document lists cumulative effects with the White Horse Reservoir as a key issue, and the developer says it is in regular dialogue with Thames Water. Whether the reservoir's Environmental Statement includes Cearn is unknown; check its cumulative long list when published
  • Construction overlap: Likely. Reservoir main works from 2029 or 2032 (Thames Water statements conflict) for over 10 years; Cearn construction would follow a decision around 2028 to 2029. Shared corridors probably include the A338 through the Hanneys, the A417 and the A415. Cearn routes not yet published
  • Who has raised it: Vale Leader Bethia Thomas (23 Oct 2026), Cllr Sally Whiteman's council question on combined impact (about 5,000 ha), the amended Vale motion (cumulative impact with Botley West, West Oxfordshire Energy Park and the reservoir), Herald Series coverage quoting county and district councillors, and Protect the Vale (combined figure of at least 5,300 acres). GARD has not linked the two
  • App guidance: Show each project in its own section. Add a shared 'Combined impacts' page covering traffic corridors, construction timing, land take and the councils' cumulative-impact positions. Residents may oppose one project and not the other; letters and actions should be project-specific, with an optional combined-impact paragraph
