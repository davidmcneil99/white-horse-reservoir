# Cearn Solar Farm: project context

Background for the solar section of the tracker app. Status as at 1 November 2026. Developer figures are claims, not assessed facts.

## The scheme

- **Name**: Cearn Solar Farm. Cearn is an old name for the River Ock.
- **Applicant**: Cearn Solar Farm Limited, a wholly owned subsidiary of Telis Energy UK, which is part of Telis Energy Group. Protect the Vale says Telis was set up and is backed by Carlyle. Consultants and landowners have not been published.
- **Size**: a grid connection offer of up to 500MW. The developer says that could power up to 143,000 homes. The site is about 2,800 acres (about 1,133 ha), in three development areas. Not all of that land would carry panels.
- **Location**: south of Garford, east of Charney Bassett, north of West Hanney, south of Goosey and north-east of Baulking. The Vale names Garford, Charney Bassett, West Hanney, Goosey and Baulking as the affected parishes. Cllr Rayner's council question says residents fear the scheme would surround Lyford.
- **Components**: solar panels, an on-site substation, battery energy storage (lithium iron phosphate expected), collector compounds, access tracks, fencing and landscaping.
- **Grid connection**: an underground cable about 12 km north-east to National Grid's proposed Farmoor 400kV substation, which is also needed for Botley West. The route will be shown at Stage 2. The parishes it would pass through are not confirmed; candidates include Lyford, Frilford, Kingston Bagpuize, Longworth, Hinton Waldrist, Appleton, Besselsleigh and Cumnor (Farmoor).
- **Land**: partly provisionally Best and Most Versatile. The amended Vale motion describes it as mostly Grade 2 and 3a.
- **Flood risk**: mostly Flood Zone 1, with some parcels in Flood Zones 2 and 3 along the Ock and its tributaries.
- **Not yet published**:
  - construction period, access routes, lorry numbers, compounds and working hours;
  - operational lifespan (comparable schemes propose about 40 years);
  - battery size;
  - the value of the community benefit fund.

## How it is decided

- **Route**: Cearn is a Nationally Significant Infrastructure Project, because solar schemes over 100MW go through the national process. The developer applies for a Development Consent Order to the Planning Inspectorate.
- **Who decides**: the Planning Inspectorate examines the application and recommends. The Secretary of State for Energy Security and Net Zero decides; that is currently Ed Miliband.
- **Who does not decide**:
  - The Vale of White Horse is only a statutory consultee and will write a Local Impact Report. Its planning committee and speaking rules do not apply.
  - Defra is not the decision-maker for Cearn. It is for the reservoir, which is a common source of confusion; the Vale's own motion asks for a letter to Defra.
- **Consultation rules**: since 24 July 2026, developers no longer have a legal duty to consult before applying. Cearn says it will still run a Stage 2 public consultation.

## Positions so far

- **Vale of White Horse**:
  - Leader Bethia Thomas supports renewables in principle, but has many unanswered questions about farmland, nature, construction traffic (especially alongside the reservoir) and community benefits. She objects to the decision being taken centrally.
  - The amended motion of 21 October 2026 says solar should go on non-agricultural land first and avoid high-grade farmland. It wants shared ownership or local benefit, and says cumulative impact with Botley West, the West Oxfordshire Energy Park and the reservoir must be considered. It also asks for a Vale renewable energy policy.
- **Councillors**:
  - Lee Evans proposed the motion, but said it was hijacked by an amendment that removed his rooftop and industrial-site point.
  - Jill Rayner and Sally Whiteman asked about residents' voices and combined impacts.
  - James Plumb prefers solar on rooftops.
  - Ron Batstone is worried about the landscape impact.
- **MPs**: Olly Glover, Charlie Maynard and Layla Moran have made no statement found as at 1 November 2026.
- **Charney Bassett Parish Council**: working with neighbouring parishes and signposting Protect the Vale. It has taken no formal position.
- **Cumnor Parish Council**: attended a Co:Design workshop and keeps a standing item on large solar on Green Belt land.
- **CPRE Oxfordshire**: monitoring the scheme. Its policy is to avoid Grades 1, 2 and 3a land, put rooftops and brownfield sites first, and treat cumulative impact as critical.
- **Protect the Vale**: opposes Cearn because of the scale of land lost in one area, not solar in principle.

## Links to the White Horse Reservoir

- **Separate projects**: Cearn and the reservoir have separate promoters, separate decision-makers (DESNZ for Cearn, Defra for the reservoir) and separate examinations.
- **Not a replacement**: Cearn is not the reservoir's solar relocation scheme. Thames Water dropped its plan to relocate the three solar farms it would remove.
- **Overlap of sites**: not established. Cearn lies north and west of West Hanney, and the reservoir lies east of the A338. Cearn's cable corridor could cross roads near the reservoir. That can be confirmed when the Stage 2 route is published, by overlaying both Planning Inspectorate boundary files.
- **Cumulative assessment**:
  - Cearn lists cumulative effects with the reservoir as a key issue, and says it is in regular dialogue with Thames Water.
  - Whether the reservoir's Environmental Statement includes Cearn is not known.
- **Construction overlap**: likely.
  - The reservoir's main works would run for over ten years from 2029 or 2032 (Thames Water's own statements conflict).
  - Cearn's construction would follow a decision in about 2028 or 2029.
  - Shared roads probably include the A338 through the Hanneys, the A417 and the A415.
- **Who has linked them so far**:
  - the Vale Leader, the amended motion, Cllr Whiteman and Herald Series coverage of local councillors;
  - Protect the Vale, which gives a combined figure of at least 5,300 acres.
  - GARD has not linked the two.

## How residents can take part

1. **Now to 23 November 2026 (scoping)**: residents cannot respond to the Planning Inspectorate directly. Ask your parish clerk and Vale ward councillor to press for these to be covered:
   - field-level land grades;
   - viewpoints from village conservation areas;
   - lorry routes that avoid villages;
   - a combined assessment with the reservoir's construction programme;
   - battery siting and fire safety.
2. **Now**:
   - Sign up for Planning Inspectorate email updates for EN0110044.
   - Contact the developer: info@cearnsolarfarm.co.uk, 08000 129 168, or Freepost CEARN SOLAR FARM.
   - Join Protect the Vale's updates.
3. **February to March 2027**: respond to Stage 2. This is the main chance to shape the layout and the cable route.
4. **After acceptance (late 2027)**: register as an Interested Party with a Relevant Representation. Only Interested Parties can take full part in the examination.
5. **During the examination**:
   - make written submissions;
   - speak at open floor hearings;
   - press the Vale and the county council for strong Local Impact Reports.
6. **At any time**: write to MPs, councillors and the DESNZ Secretary of State. Ministerial letters go by post to the Ministerial Correspondence Team, Level 3, 55 Whitehall, London SW1A 2HP. Stick to planning matters:
   - landscape and heritage setting;
   - farmland grades;
   - flood risk;
   - traffic and noise;
   - ecology;
   - battery safety;
   - cumulative effects.
7. **After a decision**: a judicial review must be brought within six weeks, under section 118 of the Planning Act 2008. It can only be on legal or procedural grounds. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Model campaign

Stop Botley West, which opposes the 840MW scheme near Oxford, is a useful model. It is:

- a volunteer-run company limited by guarantee, funded by donations;
- organised with parish liaison;
- built around a supporter mailing list;
- coordinated so that supporters, registered as Interested Parties, make submissions at each examination deadline.
