Combined Impacts

The White Horse Reservoir (PINS WA010005, decided by the Secretary of State for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs) and the Cearn Solar Farm (PINS EN0110044, decided by the Secretary of State for Energy Security and Net Zero) are separate projects with separate promoters and decision-makers. This page covers the ways in which they interact through cumulative impacts on traffic, construction timing and land take. Letters and objections should be directed to the relevant project.

GARD's campaign focuses on the White Horse Reservoir only. This page is independent of both GARD and Protect the Vale.

The two construction programmes are likely to overlap. The exact timing depends on decisions that have not yet been made and on Thames Water resolving the conflict between its own published statements.

Shared roads

Construction traffic for both projects is likely to use some of the same roads. Probable shared corridors include the A338 through the Hanneys, the A417 and the A415. not yet confirmed -- Cearn construction routes have not been published. They are expected to be set out in the Environmental Statement submitted with the Development Consent Order application, forecast for September 2027.

Combined land take

Protect the Vale puts the combined land take of Cearn, the reservoir and its associated pipeline at least 5,300 acres. claimed (Protect the Vale figure, November 2026)

This figure has not been independently verified and does not account for land that may overlap between the two schemes.
